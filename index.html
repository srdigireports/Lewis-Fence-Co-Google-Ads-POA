Executive Summary

This is our full audit and plan of action for the Lewis Fence Co. Google Ads account, built directly against the items you outlined. Before proposing changes, we pulled 90 days of live account data — every campaign, keyword, search term, and conversion action currently running — so this plan is based on what the account is actually doing.

The short version: the account is generating leads, but inefficiently — with budget leaking to broad-match drift, out-of-area clicks, and a conversion tracking setup very likely overstating "primary" conversions with low-intent signals. Exactly what a structured rebuild is for.

The headline numbers, last 90 days

$8,354.72 total spend across the account. $7,903.51 of that ran through a single, undifferentiated "Search-1" campaign on broad match. At least $1,617.26 went to search terms that produced zero conversions, and $527.54 went to clicks from Reno, Carson City, Sparks, and Elko, Nevada — all outside your service area. Every one of the account's 7 conversion actions is currently flagged "primary," including two overlapping, loosely-defined "Contact" actions likely counting page visits alongside real leads.

Time-Sensitive

One external deadline sits inside this plan: Google enforces a bidding change on Aug 17, 2026 — 10 days from today — that directly affects Search-1's Target CPA. Details in Part 1.5.

Root Cause Found

Search-1's location setting shows ads to anyone Google thinks is interested in your area — not just people physically there. Campaign Tahoe is already set correctly.

Presence or Interest → Presence Only

This one-setting fix, applied account-wide, should eliminate the Nevada leak entirely.

Separately: 39 distinct search terms each cost more than $20 and produced zero conversions, totaling $1,617.26 — about 20% of Search-1's spend. Most are competitor business names picked up by broad match (see Part 2). A handful of DIY/supply terms your own list anticipated — Home Depot, Lowe's, wholesale — are also present, at low volume so far.

That $272.53 blended figure includes whatever mix of real leads and low-intent "Contact" page-visits is currently feeding the algorithm — so the true cost of a qualified lead isn't known yet. Fix tracking (Part 3.1) first, then this number becomes trustworthy.

10 Days Out — Aug 17, 2026

Google's Own Bidding Change Lands Before Phase 0 Is Finished

Google announced in June that starting August 17, 2026, Target CPA and Target ROAS bidding stops "coasting" on found efficiency for budget-limited campaigns and instead delivers more strictly to the literal number sitting in the target field. Search-1 is flagged "Limited by budget" today, which puts it directly in scope — and Campaign Tahoe runs the same $200 target, so it's governed by the new behavior going forward too.

Normally we'd wait for Phase 0 tracking cleanup before touching the $200 target, since today's number is still mixed with weak "Contact" conversions. But the enforcement date is 10 days away, not weeks — so we recommend a provisional target check now, using Google's Bid Target Adjustment Tool (live since July 6), with a second, cleaner pass once Phase 0 data lands and the target can be set from real qualified-lead cost.

Applies to Search-1 — already budget-limited Google will not adjust targets automatically

The Plan of Action

ORGANIZED AGAINST YOUR LIST, IN THE ORDER WE RECOMMEND EXECUTING IT.

0
Phase 0 — Before Anything Else

3.1  Conversion Tracking Fix

  • Retire the duplicate "Contact" / "Contact (1)" actions, or demote to secondary — neither should influence bidding.
  • Confirm/set a 60–90 second minimum duration on "Phone calls website."
  • Test the /estimate/ form end-to-end to confirm it fires on real submission, not page load.
  • Add click-to-text and booked-appointment conversion actions.
  • Re-mark only real, qualified actions as "primary."
1

3.2  Campaigns Split by Service Area

Two fully separate campaigns — each with its own budget, ads, keywords, locations, and landing page: Placer County (Colfax, Auburn, Meadow Vista, Loomis, Rocklin, Roseville, Lincoln, Granite Bay) rebuilt from Search-1's proven keywords, and Lake Tahoe (Tahoe City, Homewood, Tahoma, Carnelian Bay, Kings Beach, Truckee, Meyers, South Lake Tahoe) expanded from the existing Campaign Tahoe. Both move to Presence-only targeting.

2

3.3  Ad Groups Split by Fence Type

Dedicated ad groups inside each area campaign — Redwood & Wood Privacy, Chain-Link, Ranch/Split-Rail/No-Climb, Ornamental Iron & Steel, Repair & Replacement, Commercial, Gates — replacing the single "Ad group 1" holding all ~24 keywords together today.

3

3.4  Keyword Strategy — High Intent First

Rebuilt around exact- and phrase-match, high-intent terms — your list plus proven performers already surfacing in the account. Broad match won't disappear (fence installation is genuinely converting), but moves into a controlled, monitored role with tight negatives underneath.

4

3.5  Negative Keywords

Your list goes in immediately. We'll add the Nevada out-of-area cities as a backstop, plus a reviewed decision on the real competitor names from Part 2 — lean in where converting (Fantastic Fence), reconsider where not (Aguilar). Nothing that looks like a legitimate customer gets excluded.

5

3.6  Ad Relevance & Copy

Ads written specifically for what was searched, landing on a matching page — headlines by area (Placer County Fence Contractor), fence type (Redwood Fence Installation), trust (Licensed C-13, Family-Owned, 5-Year Warranty), and offer (Free On-Site Estimates).

6

3.7  Dedicated Landing Pages

Built by StellarRank as part of this engagement, prioritized by search volume — Redwood/Wood Privacy and Chain-Link first, then Ranch, Ornamental, Repair, Commercial, Gates, then the two area pages. Each with a clear phone number, estimate button, project photos, reviews, license and warranty info, and a short contact form.

7

3.8  Mobile Performance

As each landing page goes live, audited for mobile load speed and above-the-fold visibility of the phone number, text button, and estimate button — folded into the same build.

8

3.9  Bidding & Campaign Goals — Revisited

Target CPA is already the right strategy account-wide — it just needs to optimize against real leads instead of a blended mix that includes page visits. Once tracking is clean, we'll re-check the $200 target against actual qualified cost-per-lead, and only lift the current budget cap once the extra spend is confirmed to buy qualified leads.
